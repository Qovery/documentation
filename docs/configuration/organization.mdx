An organization is a shared account where developers can collaborate across many projects. Organization owners and admins can manage all aspects of the organization, from clusters to member access.

## Prerequisites

To follow this section, you must have [created an account](https://console.qovery.com/signup).

## Creating an Organization

### When Signing Up

When you sign up for Qovery, you will be asked to create your first organization and project. You will need to:

1. Sign in through your Git provider (GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket), Google or Microsoft
2. Fill in your profile: full name, a work email (personal email domains are rejected), and company name. Submitting creates your organization and a first project automatically
3. Choose how to set up your first cluster: connect your own cloud account if you have cloud admin access, or set up a local demo cluster otherwise

You start on the Scale plan during your trial. For more information on available plans, visit the [pricing page](https://www.qovery.com/pricing).

Qovery also has a Free plan, with no time limit and no credit card: 3 users plus unlimited AI agents, 1 cluster of your own, and 1,000 deployment minutes a month with a hard stop: at the limit, new deployments pause until next month or until you upgrade, and Free never bills overage. It includes preview environments and environment cloning, with community support. See the [pricing page](https://www.qovery.com/pricing) for the current plans and limits.

## Change an Organization

If you have access to multiple organizations, you can switch between them:

1. Locate the organization dropdown in the header
2. Click on the dropdown to see all available organizations
3. Select the organization you want to switch to

## Delete an Organization

<Warning>
  Deleting an organization is **irreversible**. All data associated with the
  organization will be permanently deleted.
</Warning>

To delete an organization:

1. Navigate to Organization Settings
2. Scroll to the "Danger Zone" section
3. Click "Delete Organization"
4. Confirm the deletion by typing the organization name

## Billing

Manage your organization's billing and subscription:

- **View Invoices**: Access all past and current invoices
- **Payment Methods**: Add or update credit cards and payment details
- **Subscription**: Upgrade or downgrade your plan
- **Usage**: Monitor resource usage and costs

During the trial, add a credit card to activate your organization on one of the **Starter**, **Growth**, or **Scale** plans, billed monthly once the trial ends. You can switch between these plans until the trial ends.

<Info>
When you create an organization or add a payment card, you must provide your billing details, including a billing address. A **VAT number** is required for billing addresses in EU countries and optional elsewhere. Postal codes are validated against the expected format for the selected country.
</Info>

For pricing details, visit the [pricing page](https://www.qovery.com/pricing).

## Agents

The **Agents** tab gathers in one place how your team's coding agents work with Qovery. Open it from your organization's tabs, next to **Audit Logs**. The organization overview also shows a card that leads there until you dismiss it. Everything about agents lives here: the old Organization settings pages for the Copilot, skills, skill presets, agent activity, policy tokens and the Agent group redirect to it.

The idea behind it: agents can do real infrastructure work on their own when every operation is checked at the API. Agents sets that up in three parts: **Guide** agents with the Copilot and skills, tailored per team and per person with skill presets; **Control** them with policy tokens, so each agent gets exactly the permissions its job needs, checked on every API call and denied by default; and **Observe** what they did and what was blocked, in Activity and the audit logs.

Its side menu has four entries, then a **Settings** group for Owners and Admins:

- **Overview**: the setup and, once agents are in use, what they did. The setup has three steps, with the next one highlighted and one button for it: create a custom skill preset (from a template per user profile), limit what agents can do (create a policy token), and connect your agent. The introduction shows in full only on the first visit. A **Connect an agent** button at the top of the page opens the connection setup at any time, for the next laptop or CI runner. Once agents are connected and the organization has a preset and a policy token, the page shows the period's numbers (agent actions, denied actions, members using agents, skill loads) compared with the previous period, the active presets, the policy tokens in use, the latest denied actions, and suggestions. If AI features are off for the organization, a notice says so (see [Turn on AI](#turn-on-ai)).
- **Custom skill presets**: the Console label for [skill presets](/copilot/skill-presets) (Preview), your organization's own version of the Qovery skills. Create, edit and assign presets, see their history and usage, which skills members' agents loaded, and a **Preview** tab that shows what a member or a role receives.
- **Automate**: agents that run on their own, on a schedule or on an event. Each one gets a policy token that allows exactly its job: anything else is refused at the API, and every call is logged. The jobs are the [agent policy examples](/configuration/organization/policy-examples/overview), in one grid with a level filter: All, Investigate, Hygiene, Cost, Reliability and Change. One-shot operations are not listed here. Each job has a suggested trigger, such as a webhook when an incident fires or `0 8,20 * * 1-5` to stop idle environments in the evening and start them in the morning. Its panel first says where the agent runs: as an Agent Task on your Qovery infrastructure, or from a coding agent on a laptop or in CI. It then shows the prompt for the agent, what the agent can and can never do, and the steps to run it on its own: create the policy token, prefilled with the example's name, description and policy (replace the `REPLACE_WITH_` IDs, and keep the token value, shown once), then schedule the agent, in one of two ways. The first is the coding agent you already use (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode, Gemini CLI, VS Code Copilot, or any agent with the Qovery skills), signed in with the token outside the prompt (in `QOVERY_CLI_ACCESS_TOKEN` or the MCP header) and started by the agent's own scheduling, a CI cron job or the event. The second, recommended, is an [Agent Task](/configuration/agent-tasks/overview) on Qovery, with the Qovery MCP server authenticated by that token: it runs next to your services on its own schedule or webhook, with no laptop or CI to keep running. The page creates nothing for this route: **Open Automations in an environment** takes you to where you add the agent task. The page lists the Agent Tasks templates under **Or run agents on Qovery**. **Automate a new job** turns a recurring job you describe into a `/qovery-policy-token` command for your coding agent, then you schedule it the same way. See [Run it on its own](/configuration/organization/policy-examples/overview#run-it-on-its-own).
  - **Optimize builds**, under Automate: an agent with one job, finding why a Docker build is slow and how to make it faster. The page explains how to run the [Build Optimizer](/copilot/capabilities/build-optimizer) from a service's build logs, the permissions it needs and an example result.
- **Activity**: one view of what members' agents did (through the skills and the MCP server) and what policy tokens did, allowed and denied. Its menu entry shows a badge with the number of denied calls in the last 7 days. One toolbar filters by period, actor (everyone, all members, all policy tokens, or one member or token), skill, source and outcome. The filters are kept in the URL, so you can share a filtered view. **Recent actions** sits right under the chart, 30 per page, with an **Action type** filter. Break the activity down by member, by token or by skill, and click a row to filter the whole page. **What was blocked** lists the refused calls, with **Show only denied**. See [Activity](/copilot/skill-presets#activity).
- **Settings** (Owners and Admins):
  - **Policy tokens**: the organization's [API Policy Tokens](/configuration/organization/api-policy-token), managed entirely here. The Overview's setup step, the **Create policy token** buttons in Automate and the token links in Activity also lead here. **Create policy token** opens the form. Each token shows its role, a bar of its last 7 days (allowed and denied calls per day), when it was last used and when it expires, with a **No expiry** warning badge for a token that never expires. Its row menu has **See activity** (Activity filtered on that token), **View policy** (read-only: a policy cannot change after creation) and **Delete token** (you confirm by typing the token's name). Deleted tokens that made calls in the last 30 days are listed greyed out.
  - **Copilot settings** (Beta): what the [Console AI Copilot](/copilot/console) does, its status, and its settings: access source, access mode, scheduled tasks, and **Turn off Copilot**. A notice says the Copilot will change: troubleshooting and optimization are moving into dedicated features, starting with Optimize builds.
  - **Model tokens** and **MCP servers**, where [Agent Tasks](/configuration/agent-tasks/overview) are available, shared with every agent task in the organization. See [Agent Tasks: model tokens and MCP servers](#agent-tasks-model-tokens-and-mcp-servers).

**Connect** is not in the menu. It opens from the **Connect an agent** button and from the last setup step. It shows which preset your agent loads ("Your agent loads your organization's preset", with its name, or "No preset yet: agents get the full Qovery skills"), the commands to install the [Qovery agent skills](/getting-started/quickstart/agent-skills) and the [Qovery CLI](/cli/overview), the setup of the [Qovery MCP server](/copilot/mcp-server) with a tab per coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI and VS Code), and the list of Qovery skills.

An agent counts as connected once the organization has at least one skill load, agent action or denied call in the last 30 days.

**Who sees what**

| Page | Owners and Admins | Other members |
|------|-------------------|---------------|
| Overview | The setup, or the organization's numbers once set up | **Your agent setup**: what their agent receives and their own activity, read-only |
| Custom skill presets | Create, edit and assign | Read-only view of what their agent receives |
| Automate | Yes, with **Create policy token** | Read-only: **Copy prompt** and links, and who can create the token |
| Optimize builds | Yes, with **Turn on AI** when AI features are off | Yes. When AI features are off, they are told to ask an Owner or Admin |
| Activity | The whole organization, members and policy tokens | **Your activity**: their own, with no actor filter and no token breakdown |
| Connect | Yes | Yes |
| Settings | Policy tokens, Copilot settings, model tokens and MCP servers | Not in the menu |
| Settings → Policy tokens | Create, view and delete tokens, with each token's last 7 days | Not in the menu |

### Turn on AI

AI features are the organization's opt-in to AI. The Copilot needs them, and so does the security review that checks every skill preset before it is saved. New organizations have AI features on by default.

When AI features are off, the Agents Overview shows a notice, **AI features are off for this organization**. Owners and Admins turn everything on in one step:

1. Click **Turn on AI** in the notice.
2. Read the summary of the Copilot terms, open the full terms if you want, accept them and confirm.

This one confirmation turns on the organization's AI features and the Copilot, in read-only mode. To allow actions such as deploys and restarts, change the access mode later in **Settings → Copilot settings**. If the Copilot cannot be turned on, AI features stay on and you can retry from the same page.

Other members cannot turn on AI.

### Agent Tasks: model tokens and MCP servers

<AgentTasksEvolutionNote />

Under **Agents → Settings**, Owners and Admins manage what's shared with every [Agent Task](/configuration/agent-tasks/overview) in the organization: AI provider tokens under **Model tokens**, and MCP servers under **MCP servers**.

**Model tokens**: create and manage the AI provider tokens used by agent tasks. Qovery currently supports [Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/) (Anthropic), [AWS Bedrock](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/), and Anthropic-compatible gateways as providers. See [Choose a Provider and Model](/configuration/agent-tasks/overview#creating-an-agent) for the Anthropic-compatible Base URL and limits. A token created here, or created on the fly from an agent task's **Choose a Provider** step, is saved to this list and can be reused by any other agent task in the organization instead of re-entering credentials each time.

**MCP servers**: connect any remote HTTPS MCP server here (name, URL, and headers) to make it available when configuring an individual agent.

<Info>
Qovery's own MCP server is added here automatically, organization-scoped and read-only, as soon as any agent task adds a Qovery service as Context, see [Add MCP Servers](/configuration/agent-tasks/overview#creating-an-agent). To add it yourself instead, for example with write access, use `https://mcp.qovery.com/mcp` and authenticate with an [API Policy Token](/configuration/organization/api-policy-token) (a regular API token works too).
</Info>

### General Information

Configure basic organization details:

- **Company name**: Your organization's official name
- **Description**: Brief description of your organization
- **Website**: Your company website URL
- **Admin contact emails**: Enter one or multiple email addresses separated by commas

<Info>
  Admin contact emails are used for communications about infrastructure outages,
  maintenance updates, and weekly and monthly usage reports.
</Info>

Click **Update** to save your changes.

### Moved to Agents

The Copilot, skills, skill presets, agent activity (now **Activity**), policy tokens and the **Agent** group (model tokens and MCP servers for Agent Tasks) are no longer in Organization settings. They are in the [Agents](#agents) tab, and the old settings links redirect there. Regular [API tokens](/configuration/organization/api-token) stay in Organization settings, under **API token**.
